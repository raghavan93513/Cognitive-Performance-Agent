# CMO Steal File

From the CMO seat, for the team. The brief: understand everything in the steal library from the CMO's point of view before we start. This file covers how each product was **positioned, launched and grown, and how it talks**. It also looks at what that means for our five deadlines:
- the **Nov 6** proposal;
- the **Nov 11** written draft;
- the **Dec 11** showcase (a 1-minute pitch, a poster and a live demo);
- recruiting classmates for the pilot;
- whatever comes after the course.

**How to read it.** A number in square brackets, like [12], is a source in section G that I opened on 2026-11-04. A fact with no bracket comes from a vault page, linked in the prose. `^[inferred]` marks my judgement. `[unverified]` means the source is weak or secondary. Proposed copy is marked **for the team to lock**: the team decides, the CTO checks technical claims, and the CSO checks study and consent wording.

Read with [[09-design/steal-library|Steal library]], [[24-synthesis/market-map|Market map]], [[24-synthesis/casebook|Casebook]] and [[13-team/exec-team|AI exec team]].

## A. The steal library through the CMO's eyes

The library is a design tool: steal the mechanism, not the look. Each mechanism is also a **story**. It says something about us that a grader, a classmate or a future user can repeat. Below, each card gets a line on the story it lets us tell and a warning. All 12 cards are still `Seen: described`. Where I researched the company behind a card, I say so; the cards themselves aren't mine to upgrade.

| Card | The story it lets us tell | Warning for our story |
|---|---|---|
| S-01 Google Flights range bar | "Compared with *your* usual, not a stranger's" | Only true after the learning period. Before then, the honest line is "still learning your usual" |
| S-02 Apple Vitals, quiet until several signals are off | "It stays quiet unless something is really different" | Silence is invisible in a demo. We have to *narrate* it ("it stayed quiet for 40 minutes, on purpose") |
| S-03 MyChart, a value plus who to talk to | "It knows when to point you to a person" (graded) | One word too clinical crosses the FDA line (casebook B1). The escalation copy needs a CSO check |
| S-04 Perplexity, numbered sources | "Every reason has a receipt" | Perplexity's citations didn't always back the sentence. In generative search, only 74.5% did (Liu 2023, in Design evidence §3). A marker must open a real record, or it's theatre |
| S-05 Apple Weather, "rain stopping in 12 min" | "It talks like a forecast: likely, fairly sure" | A forecast that's wrong in front of graders hurts. Rehearse the abstain path as hard as the happy path |
| S-06 Garmin, factors behind readiness | "You can see what went into it" | Drop the overall score in the story too. Never say "your readiness" |
| S-07 Rise, an energy curve | The positioning foil: Rise plans your day; we react to the session you're in | Rise is the closest story to ours. If we sound like "an energy app", we lose the contrast |
| S-08 Gentler Streak, rest counts | "Saying no to a break is a respected answer" | Calm copy can read as bland. The story still needs one sharp line |
| S-09 BeReal, once a day | "It speaks once, then gets out of your way" | BeReal's ritual didn't keep people (case 3). Use it for notification restraint, not as a retention promise |
| S-10 Citymapper, one next step | "One clear suggestion, alternatives one tap away" | Not a story by itself. It supports the card |
| S-11 Live Activities | The most memorable frame for a phone audience | Needs a native iOS app (open decision #4). Don't put it in the Nov 6 story unless it's built |
| S-12 Spotify Wrapped, a shareable recap | The only place we may be bold: a weekly card a student might forward | Wrapped shares count screenshots [4]. Any value on the card can be shared, so it must carry **no health values at all**, not only in a "share version" |

**Study-list products, from the CMO's side.** I researched how these were launched and how they talk; details are in section B.
- **WHOOP and Oura:** sold an outcome and a daily moment.
- **Duolingo:** try before signup, then a voice that works on TikTok but turns into guilt in the product.
- **one sec, Opal, Forest:** "focus" sells to students; "mental health" didn't convert Gen Z for Opal [19].
- **Finch and How We Feel:** shame-free tone, and credibility borrowed from science.
- **Strava:** clubs and your own history.
- **Perplexity:** "answers with sources" as a whole positioning.
- **Apple:** privacy as a headline.

**What the library adds up to, as a story.** The library's own synthesis is Google Flights' "your usual range", plus Perplexity's sources, plus BeReal's restraint. Told as a story, that becomes: ^[inferred]

> **"Keep going, walk, or break? It shows why, and says when it isn't sure."** (for the team to lock)

The anti-steals (the lone readiness ring, red/green verdicts, streak flames, notification spam, mascots at serious moments, purple "AI wellness") are also the **marketing** anti-steals. Each one is a story we must never tell (section E).

## B. Cases

Each case gives what happened (sourced), the principle, and **the steal for us**. They're grouped by what we need them for.

### B1. Student-first launches

**1. Facebook at Harvard (2004).** About 650 registered in 24 hours and 4,300 within two weeks. It spread through a few campus email lists and dining-hall talk. Only harvard.edu addresses could join, which early users said made it feel "less sketchy". Columbia was one of the next schools, by March 2004 [28].
- *Principle:* one closed community first. The boundary itself was a trust signal.
- *Steal:* "built at Columbia, tested with Columbia students" is a credible, small frame for the pilot and the poster. ^[inferred]
- *Warning:* the spread came from mass email, and our recruiting must not (see case 20).

**2. BeReal and the paid campus ambassadors (2022).**
- Ambassadors ran events and parties and were paid per referral. Brown's paper reported $30 per referral and $50 per download with a review. The program ran January to June 2022 [1].
- 7.67M downloads in 2022 by April, with 2.93M daily users [1]; 1.7M downloads in one July week [2].
- TechCrunch reported that "at least some" of the growth "had been manufactured" to look viral [1].
- Daily users peaked around 15M in October 2022 and fell to about 6M by late spring 2023. Voodoo bought BeReal for a reported €500M in June 2024 [3].
- BeReal later loosened its own scarcity: bonus posts in 2023, raised to five in July 2024 [3].
- *Principle:* paid reach bought a spike. The once-a-day ritual didn't hold most of it.
- *Steal:* the restraint (one prompt, a short window) for notifications only.
- *Warning:* **retention before reach.** The a16z guidance in [[24-synthesis/market-map|Market map]] §3 agrees: ambassadors only after people keep using it.

**3. Locket (Jan 2022).** One developer built a home-screen photo widget as a gift for his girlfriend. A TikTok walkthrough of the screen made it the top US App Store app [29]. Joining needed address-book access [29].
- *Principle:* a 15-second screen recording that shows the whole mechanic is the shareable unit.
- *Steal:* make our demo shareable the same way. One short clip shows the whole loop: card, reason, sources, "Keep going". It doubles as the recorded fallback the course requires. ^[inferred]
- *Warning:* never ask for contacts to grow.

**4. Fizz (2021–22).**
- An anonymous campus app launched at Stanford, then 13 campuses, then 25 in under two months.
- It claimed "95% penetration among iPhone users" at some campuses; TechCrunch noted the downloads may be inflated. It gave out free donuts for downloads.
- In November 2021, three Stanford students found that anyone could query its database and identify who wrote any post. Fizz's lawyer reportedly threatened them, and the EFF stepped in [30].
- *Principle:* the privacy-minded student *will* audit you, and your reaction becomes the story.
- *Steal:* invite the audit. Publish exactly what the collector records, and thank anyone who finds a hole. ^[inferred]

**5. Gas (2022–23).**
- An anonymous compliments app for high schoolers: 5.1M downloads by early November 2022, and briefly #1 on the App Store [31].
- A rumor that it was linked to trafficking spread on TikTok and Snapchat. Police and school warnings followed, and some were later retracted. Discord bought it in January 2023 and shut it down on 7 November 2023, citing a steep fall in users [31].
- *Principle:* safety rumors outrun facts. A viral spike isn't a habit.
- *Steal:* have a plain "what we collect, who sees it, how to delete" page live **before** we ask anyone to join.

**6. Superhuman's onboarding and the 40% test.**
- Its first score was 22% "very disappointed". It segmented to the people who loved it ("Nicole"), reached 33%, then 58% within three quarters [33].
- It surveyed users who had used it at least twice in two weeks; about 40 answers is "directionally correct" [33].
- At its peak, about 20 people onboarded new users by hand (podcast summary [34]).
- *Principle:* find the people who'd miss it most, then serve them by hand.
- *Steal:*
  - a 15-minute 1:1 setup with each pilot participant (consent, sources, a first session);
  - the Ellis question at pilot exit, reported with its n and labelled directional, since our pilot is far below 40. ^[inferred]

### B2. Brands in the steal library: positioning, growth, voice

**7. Duolingo.**
- **Try before signup.** It runs the first lesson before asking for an account. Appcues reports "a 20% lift in daily active users" from that alone [6]. That's a secondary source with no method, so it's `[unverified]`.
- **The streak.** Separating the streak from the daily goal raised day-14 retention by 3.3% and the share of learners on 7+ day streaks by "over 40%" [5].
- **The voice.** The "unhinged" TikTok voice had 1.9M followers by December 2021 [7]. The February 2025 "death of Duo" stunt coincided with a 38% jump in Android downloads the next day (Similarweb) [8].
- **The cost.** Long-term users call the in-app guilt copy bullying. *The Spectator*, 22 Jun 2026, quotes a notification: "Don't let Duo down today" [9].
- *Principle:* the brand voice and the product voice can split. Duolingo's TikTok voice is fun, while its notifications use guilt.
- *Steal:* a QR code on the poster that opens the card on sample data, with **no account**.
- *Warning:* never borrow the voice. A tired student at 1 am meets the product voice, not the TikTok one.

**8. Spotify Wrapped.**
- 2025: about 200M "engaged users" in about 24 hours (+19%), where engaged means viewing at least one story.
- Shares were about 500M (+41%), **including downloads and screenshots** [4].
- *Principle:* people share what shows *them*, not the product.
- *Steal:* a weekly "how your breaks went" card that a student would share about themselves, for example "4 walks this week" (for the team to lock). It's also a good poster visual.
- *Warning:* the whole card is screenshot-able, so no sleep, HR or HRV values on it. Strava's heatmap (casebook B10) is the cautionary tale for anything aggregated.

**9. Gentler Streak.** Won Apple's 2024 Design Award for Social Impact. Apple praised its encouraging reminders, and a Monthly Summary that "emphasizes … individual progression rather than comparison against others" [10].
- *Principle:* kind copy can win the most visible design prize in its category.
- *Steal:* "compared with your own usual" as a line in the story. "Rest counts" as tone.

**10. Rise Science.**
- Founded in 2015 by two Northwestern students. The football team asked them for a sleep plan before training camp, and contracts with college and pro teams followed [11].
- Consumer beta in 2020 with "more than 1 million" downloads; public launch in summer 2021 [11]. $15.5M raised, $60 a year, and users checked it "14 out of 28 days" [12].
- It positions as energy, not sleep tracking [12].
- *Principle:* athletes for credibility, the outcome (energy) for scale.
- *Steal:* sell the outcome the science supports, **"less tired"**, never "better grades" ([[12-research/science-and-tech-evidence|Science and tech evidence]] §1: breaks reduce fatigue, d = .35; cognitive-task performance d = −.09).

**11. Oura.**
- US awareness rose from about 5% (2022) to 30%, through design, wellness, sleep as the core message, and 4,000+ retail stores [13].
- **Correction for the market map:** the Modern Retail piece I opened [13] doesn't support the [[24-synthesis/market-map|Market map]]'s line that Oura "credits mostly organic celebrity wear and word of mouth". It describes pop culture, fashion and retail. Re-check before quoting.
- **Trust lesson (Aug–Sep 2025).** One press-release phrase linked to Palantir ("population-level analysis of risk and readiness") set off **weeks** of TikTok calls to dump the ring. The CEO then had to say on stage, "We will never sell your data to anyone, ever" [14].
- *Steal:* write our one data sentence so plainly that it can't be misread. Read every partner or vendor sentence aloud before it ships. ^[inferred]

**12. WHOOP.**
- Founded by a Harvard student-athlete; a membership model since 2018 [16].
- Penn State gave every athlete a strap, and joining the team dashboard is **voluntary** [17].
- **Biggest growth moment:** in June 2020, golfer Nick Watney's respiratory-rate change preceded a positive COVID test. The PGA Tour bought 1,000 straps. WHOOP said its tech "can detect early symptoms", from research not yet peer-reviewed; the straps weren't FDA-approved [15].
- Five years later came the FDA warning letter over blood-pressure claims (casebook B1).
- *Principle:* the most viral health story is usually a detection claim, and detection claims are where regulators look.
- *Warning:* no demo line like "it caught that I was exhausted". We suggest; we don't detect.

**13. one sec.** In a PNAS field study (n = 280, 6 weeks), target-app openings fell **57%**. The **option to dismiss** "had the strongest effect"; the deliberation message alone did nothing. The app's originator is a co-author [18].
- *Principle:* the easy "no" is the active ingredient.
- *Steal:* make **"Keep going"** a hero of the demo, not a footnote. It's a sourced, counter-intuitive line graders will remember: "the easiest button is the one that ignores us" (for the team to lock). It's our Sutherland-style "illogical" option. ^[inferred]

**14. Opal.**
- In early testing, "a mental health angle for Gen Z users didn't convert, but productivity for professionals did."
- Students adopted it "organically", and it grew from $0 to $10M a year in recurring revenue (ARR) with 11 people [19]. That's a VC blog `[unverified]`.
- *Principle:* students say yes to "help me focus", not "fix my mental health".
- *Steal:* the job-to-be-done is the **study session**. Wellbeing stays the frame, not the headline. ^[inferred]

**15. Forest.** "Since 2014, over 60 million people have used Forest", and 2.1M real trees have been funded with Trees for the Future [20]. The tree dies if you leave during a strict session [20].
- *Principle:* a session that feels like something you made.
- *Warning:* the loss framing (a dead tree) is exactly the guilt we ban. Keep "you made something", drop "it died".

**16. Finch.** The bird is "not the kind of pet you need to worry about keeping alive". Finch "doesn't take the guilt approach", though a check-in streak still breaks. It has a "First Aid Kit" with helpline links [21]. User counts are `[unverified]`.
- *Steal:* help one tap away, inside a calm product.
- *Warning:* no mascot near consent or escalation.

**17. How We Feel.** A science-based nonprofit. Pinterest's co-founder led the product, and the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence led the science. The check-in is: log the feeling, tag what's behind it, get an activity [22].
- *Principle:* credibility borrowed openly from a named science partner.
- *Steal:* name our science lineage on one slide and on the poster: Kaur et al., CHI 2020 (77% of suggestions rated well-timed) and Albulescu 2022. **Named, not overstated.**

**18. Strava.** More than 120M athletes. Gen Z is "the primary source of community growth through new clubs". 77% of Gen Z feel more connected when they see friends' activity [23].
- *Principle:* the atomic network is a club, not a campus.
- *Steal after the course:* one study group or club as the first network (Chen). Not before Dec 11.

**19. Perplexity, "answers with sources".**
- Founded in Aug 2022, launched in Dec 2022, and called itself an "answer engine" that cites its sources. 2M unique visitors by Feb 2023 [24].
- Then came the Forbes plagiarism charge (June 2024), robots.txt reports, and suits from Dow Jones and the NY Post, Britannica, Reddit and others [24].
- *Principle:* "with sources" can be a whole position. It only holds if the sources are real and respected.
- *Steal:* "every reason has a receipt" as our grounding story.
- *Warning:* the receipt must be the student's own timestamped record, never a decorative chip.

**20. Apple's privacy marketing.**
- At CES in January 2019, a billboard read "what happens on your iPhone stays on your iPhone", linking to apple.com/privacy [25]. "Privacy. That's iPhone." followed.
- When App Tracking Transparency arrived, only **4%** of US users opted in to tracking (Flurry, May 2021) [26].
- Then Apple paid **$95M** to settle claims that Siri recorded people without "Hey Siri" and that contractors listened. Final approval came on 16 Oct 2025; Apple denied wrongdoing [27].
- *Principle:* asked plainly, people say no to tracking, and privacy marketing makes you the standard you'll be judged by.
- *Steal:* lead with the privacy line, keep it literally true, and have the CTO sign it off.

### B3. Wellness and health brands that won or lost on trust

**21. Flo's Anonymous Mode (2022).** After the 2021 FTC settlement over sharing health data with Facebook and Google, and after Dobbs, Flo shipped Anonymous Mode on 14 Sep 2022. It needs no name or email, and Flo said, "Women's health information shouldn't be a liability" [40].
- *Principle:* trust can be rebuilt with a feature, not a statement.
- *Steal:* make privacy something you can *use* in the demo: a pause and a delete, shown live.

**22. Mozilla's review of mental-health apps.** In 2022, 29 of 32 got a privacy warning; Mozilla said they were "worse than any other product category". In 2023, 19 of 32 still did. Cerebral set a record of 799 trackers in the first minute [43].
- *Principle:* the category starts out distrusted.
- *Steal:* "no ad or analytics trackers, anywhere" is a real differentiator, not hygiene. ^[inferred]

### B4. How course and research demos are pitched well

**23. YC's Demo Day guide.**
- Four qualities: clarity, excitement, informative, **memorable** ("later they can recall who you are and what you do").
- "More than 7 words on a slide" is likely too many.
- "Demos seldom work in modern demo day presentations."
- Avoid cumulative graphs.
- "Tell them as directly as possible what to remember" [35].
- *Steal:*
  - The 1-minute pitch has **no live demo**. It sends people to the station, which is the course's stated goal.
  - The last line of every talk says what to remember.
  - Never show a cumulative sign-up chart.

**24. Lightning-talk practice.** A strong hook, one idea, "one slide = one thought", and links for more [36].
- *Steal:* the 60-second pitch is one hook, one frame and one ask: "come see it say 'not enough data'" (for the team to lock).

**25. #betterposter.** The main finding sits "right in the middle, in plain language and big letters", the layout "looks clean, almost empty", and a QR code leads to the details [37].
- *Steal:* the poster's center is one sentence, the honest-uncertainty line. The architecture diagram (required) and results go in the side bars.

### B5. Recruiting pilot users ethically

**26. Columbia's IRB guidance on students as participants (2024)** [38]. Key points:
- Studies that target students should be **no more than minimal risk**.
- Pressure "might be perceived rather than actual".
- Recruit by "a general announcement or central posting" rather than approaching people in person.
- Extra credit needs justification and equal alternatives.
- Students must be told **who will have access to the data**, and how to contact HRPO if they feel coerced.

*Steal:*
- Our recruiting is an **announcement plus a form**, never a DM or an in-person ask from a friend.
- The consent covers who sees the data (the three of us, nobody else), and the HRPO contact goes on it.
- Whether we need IRB review is open decision #11, owned by the CSO. My copy waits for that answer.

**27. Duke's residence-hall wearable pilot** (Haga et al., *JMIR Res Protoc* 2020) [39].
- Freshmen in one hall were invited by mail and email five weeks before move-in.
- 175 invited → 120 enrolled → 114 started → 107 still active at the end of fall. Weekly survey completion among Apple Watch users was 70–96%.
- Participants **were given a wearable**, and in-person orientation was required.
- *Principle:* a defined small group, early notice, orientation and a free device give high uptake.
- *Warning:* we can't give devices, so plan for less. Treat the 3.3% median 30-day retention of mental-health apps ([[24-synthesis/market-map|Market map]] §4) as the floor, not Duke's 89%. ^[inferred]

## C. CMO principles for this project

1. **The course is the first market, and the grader is the first customer.** The grader remembers one frame ([35] "memorable"). Choose it on purpose: **the card saying "not enough data"** and still being useful. It shows grounding, honesty and safety at once, and nobody else's demo will have it. ^[inferred]
2. **Lead with the honest thing.** Two lines carry the whole position: "it tells you when it doesn't know" and "it never reads what you type, browse or say". The second needs the CTO's sign-off against the collector's real behaviour (idle time and app category only, per [[10-trust-safety/privacy-and-consent|Privacy and consent]]).
3. **Promise only "less tired".** The evidence supports fatigue, not scores (§1 of the science page). This rules out the hook on [[30-course/deliverables/part-3-final-showcase|Part 3]], *"My next quiz score went back up"*. It's an anecdote implying a cognitive effect the meta-analysis doesn't support, and something a classmate pilot "can't show" (§8). **It must go before the Nov 11 draft.**
4. **The easy "no" is the product** (one sec [18], Gentler Streak [10]). "Keep going" sits next to "Walk 10 min", at equal weight, in every frame we show.
5. **Retention before reach** (BeReal [1][3], Gas [31], the a16z guidance in the market map). No ambassadors, ads or "launch" until the pilot shows people keep it for a week. Measure behaviour (day-7 active), not applause.
6. **Smallest viable audience.** Columbia undergraduates who study 3+ hours at a laptop, **with or without a tracker** (the laptop-only path matters: plan for about half without one, per the market map). Athletes are a future segment unless decision #10 says otherwise.
7. **Every data sentence is a privacy decision** (Oura [14], Apple [27], Fizz [30]). One plain page — what we read, what we never read, who sees it, how to delete — is live before recruiting starts.
8. **Recruiting is an announcement, never a favour** (Columbia HRPO [38]). No DMs, no in-person asks, no grade link, no friendship pressure, and the HRPO contact goes on the consent.
9. **Credibility is borrowed openly and modestly** (How We Feel [22], Rise [11]). Name Kaur 2020 and Albulescu 2022, and don't stretch them.
10. **Zero pixels, first-party counts only.** Source-tagged links (the poster QR, the announcement link) are counted by our own server. No analytics SDK on the form or the demo page.
11. **Copy at grade 8, minutes over adjectives.** "Walk 10 min", not "recharge".

**Copy for the team to lock**

- **5-second line** (three variants; my pick is *sharp*):
  - *Safe:* "A study-break helper that shows its reasons and says when it doesn't know."
  - *Sharp:* "Keep going, walk, or break? It shows why, and says when it isn't sure."
  - *Bold:* "The wellness app that admits when it doesn't know."
  - Test with 3 people outside the team, using the 5-second test.
- **The 60-second lightning pitch** (beats):
  1. **Hook (0–10 s):** "4 in 10 undergrads sleep under 7 hours on weeknights. Then we study for hours anyway." (ACHA 2025: 41.9%, n = 47,194.)
  2. **What (10–25 s):** "It watches how long you've been going and how you slept, and suggests keep going, a short walk, or a break."
  3. **The frame (25–45 s):** "When it doesn't have enough data, it says so, and tells you what's missing."
  4. **Ask (45–60 s):** "Come see it say 'not enough data'. And see what it never reads."
  5. Results are said only with their n, after the CSO's check.
- **The privacy line:** "It never reads what you type, browse or say." Needs the CTO's sign-off.
- **The recruiting announcement** (draft; consent wording comes from the CSO):

  > "We're testing a study-break helper for our BINF 4070 project. It runs on your laptop during your normal study for [N] days. It records idle time and app type, like 'browser' or 'notes', and never what you type, browse or say. A watch or ring is optional. You can stop anytime. Saying no changes nothing, and nobody but our team of three sees your data. Sign up: [form]. Questions or concerns: [team contact] or Columbia's HRPO."

## E. Anti-steals and warning signs

**Never steal:**
- **Paid ambassadors, referral bounties, donuts for downloads** (BeReal [1], Fizz [30]). Inside a class, a reward plus a friend asking is pressure (HRPO [38]).
- **Detection stories** ("it caught…", "it knew before I did") (WHOOP [15], casebook B1).
- **A guilt voice in the product, even if it's funny in marketing** (Duolingo [9]).
- **Loss framing** (Forest's dying tree [20]) and **streaks**.
- **"Risk-free trial" language** or anything about renewal (Noom [42]).
- **A staged launch demo** (Humane [41]). Our recorded fallback shows the real path, abstain state included.
- **Cumulative sign-up charts and download counts** as proof (YC [35]; Akili in the casebook).
- **Contacts, address book or "invite friends"** as growth (Locket [29]).
- **Any analytics or ad SDK** on the form, demo page or poster link (Mozilla [43], casebook B2–B6).
- **Mascots or cute illustration** on consent, delete or "talk to someone".
- **The words:** "AI-powered", "smarter", "better grades", "optimise", "unlock", "clinical", "detect", "diagnose", "abnormal". Also "performance" in public copy without a definition (see F).

**Warning signs that we're drifting:**
- A slide claims anything the pilot hasn't measured, or a result appears without its n.
- The demo script has no abstain or escalate beat.
- Someone proposes posting in a group chat "just to get numbers".
- The poster's center is the architecture diagram instead of the finding.
- We start describing athletes in the pitch before decision #10 is made.
- A teammate asks a friend directly to "do us a favour" and join the pilot.
- The privacy line gets longer. Long privacy copy is usually hiding an exception.

## F. What we know / what we don't know yet

### Known (with links)

- **The problem, in numbers:** ACHA Spring 2025 (n = 47,194). 41.9% sleep under 7 hours on weeknights, and 75.5% are tired on 3+ of 7 days. Procrastination hurt academics for 48.8%, stress 38.3%, sleep 23.6%, ADHD 13.5%. See [[24-synthesis/market-map|Market map]] §2.
- **What we can promise:** less fatigue (d = .35), not better cognitive performance (d = −.09). See [[12-research/science-and-tech-evidence|Science and tech evidence]] §1.
- **What the pilot can and can't show:** it can show timing ratings, abstain rates and feasibility. It can't show learning gains or anything about athletes unless they enrol (§8).
- **Incumbents own scores;** the readiness and stress features are listed in [[24-synthesis/competitive-landscape|Competitive landscape]].
- **Retention reality:** a 3.3% median at 30 days; Akili had about 3% of downloads become active users. See [[24-synthesis/casebook|Casebook]] A13.
- **What graders reward and penalise:** see [[30-course/project-requirements|Project requirements]]. The Nov 6 format is 5 minutes plus 3 of feedback: [[30-course/deliverables/part-2-proposal-presentation|Part 2]].
- **Columbia's rules for recruiting students:** minimal risk, an announcement rather than an approach, data access disclosed, HRPO contact given [38].
- **Shame-free tone and an easy "no" have evidence or prizes behind them:** one sec [18], Gentler Streak [10].
- **Privacy, asked plainly, gets a no:** 4% of US users opted in to tracking [26].

# ICP test: the landing page hero

**Mode T.** Heramb asked: "now icp test the hero section".

**What was shown.** The hero of landing v6:
- the headline "Every long study session needs a halftime.";
- the bold problem line and its lede;
- the device line and the sign-up link;
- the "When is it?" demo.

**The walk, four frames per device:**
1. the first screen as it loads (treated as 5 seconds);
2. one scroll;
3. a tap on Late night;
4. a tap on After practice.

**Devices:** each tester on their own. **Captures:** `.icp-tests/2026-11-05-hero/` (gitignored).

**Synthetic personas and synthetic data: a rehearsal, never evidence.**

## Headline

**4 MEH, 1 I'D USE IT.** Sam: "This is because of the Late night chip, not the headline. The headline alone is a MEH."

- **The hero explains the product.** All five knew in 5 seconds that it tells you when to take a study break, and why. All five were sure. Clarity averaged **8.0**. Four would scroll and one would tap a chip. Nobody would leave, and nobody tapped the joining link.
- **It doesn't tell each person it's for them.** Each tester's single fix is about their own case:
  - **Maya:** Apple Watch isn't named.
  - **Diego:** Garmin isn't named, and nothing says "your coach never sees it".
  - **Anika:** nothing says what the laptop app records, on which OS, or whether an AI sees it.
  - **Jordan:** "no watch" is the smallest, greyest line, and the demo opens on a watch user.
  - **Sam:** the words are about a 3 pm lab, not about stopping for the night.

## What they said

**The headline.** "Every long study session needs a halftime." landed for **1 of 5** (Diego, a soccer player).
- Maya: a halftime is "a break on a timer", which is what made her quit Pomodoro.
- Anika: "'Every' overclaims, and 'needs' is a verdict." It also contradicts "when one fits".
- Sam: "At 1 am I don't need a halftime. I need someone to say 'you're done.'"
- Jordan: it reads as "athletes first".
- **3 of 5 rewrote it as a question:**
  - Jordan: "Push on, or stop? Halftime tells you, and why."
  - Anika: "Push on, or stop? See the reason first."
  - Sam: "Keep going, or call it a night?"

**The bold line.** "…it's hard to tell if you need a break or should push on" is **everyone's problem** (5 of 5).
- Maya: "my hour-3 question, word for word." Anika: "nearly word for word."
- "Two hours into studying" fits Maya, Anika and Diego, but not Jordan ("done 25 minutes in" after a shift) or Sam (midnight).

**"Always shows you why"** worked for all five.

**On the iPhone**
- 5 of 5: the card's reason is below the fold or cut off. Maya and Anika say the headline takes about a quarter to a third of the screen.
- 4 of 5: their thumb goes to the "When is it?" chips first.

## Fixed after the test (landing v7)

- **The night card's hours are now worked out.** "leaves you 8 h 50 min" at 12:40 and "7 h 50 min" at 1:40, replacing a fixed "8 h" and "7 h". Caught by Anika and Sam.
- **The laptop card resizes instantly once the fonts load,** instead of animating its width. It was caught cut off mid-animation on the first screen (Jordan). Checked in `.crits/2026-11-05-landing-r10/`.
- **Hero columns are top-aligned,** so the headline no longer jumps when the card changes height (Jordan).

## Fixes, ranked by how many named them

**Copy the page can carry:**
1. **Say what "1:30" and "close" are, in the card's header** (5): e.g. "50 min to bed (1:30)" and "80 min to library close". Sam asks who set 1:30.
2. **Rename "No watch" under the sleep question** (4): e.g. ask "Watch or ring?" first, or use a chip reading "Not tracked".
3. **Make the joining link plainer** (3): e.g. "Not open yet. What joining involves →", not styled like a sign-up.
4. **Headline as the question** (3): "Push on, or stop?" family. **Team call:** the name "Halftime" stays either way.
5. **Bold line names the laptop and no watch** (Jordan, Anika), **and stopping for the night** (Sam, with the boardroom's late-night lead).
6. **Put the "Sample" label above the card** (2).

**Needs the team:**
7. **The device line:** which devices to name (Apple Watch for Maya, Garmin for Diego), and whether to say "coming soon". Only Fitbit is confirmed (boardroom on privacy).
8. **A hero line on what the laptop app records and where it goes** (Anika). It waits on the "Where it goes" lines from the privacy boardroom.
9. **"Your coach never sees it"** (Diego). It needs the true answer on who sees the data.
10. **The demo's default** (Afternoon vs Late night, and watch vs no watch). This is the boardroom's decision 1.

**"If you fix only one thing"** (each tester's):
- Maya: name the Apple Watch, or say "coming soon".
- Diego: name Garmin, or say "not yet", plus "your coach never sees it".
- Anika: one hero line on what the laptop app records, on which OS, and where it goes, including whether an AI sees it.
- Jordan: open the demo on "No watch", and put "No watch needed" in the bold line.
- Sam: the headline and bold line should include stopping for the night.

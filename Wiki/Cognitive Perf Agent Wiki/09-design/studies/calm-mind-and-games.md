# Study — Calm apps and calm games

> Studied by the UIUX seat on 2026-11-05 from public sources (no accounts). "Seen: researched" = docs, store pages, reviews and articles read, not hands-on.

This page goes deeper on the "Calm and mental health" and "Calm games" rows of the [[09-design/steal-library|Steal library]] study list. The escalation and dismissal research already in [[09-design/design-evidence|Design evidence]] (the Wysa 2.4% helpline figure, Headspace's deletion trouble, the three-rung ladder) is not repeated here.

## Takeaways for our screens

1. **Suggestion card tone: name the state, never judge it.** How We Feel's science partners frame feelings as neither good nor bad, only something to manage ([Yale Medicine](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/the-how-we-feel-app-helping-emotions-work-for-us-not-against-us/)). Reviewers single out its lack of guilt for skipped suggestions ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384?see-all=reviews)). → The card's reason line describes ("2 h 40 min on 5 h sleep"). It never grades ("poor", "bad night"). ^[inferred]
2. **Break timer and breathing: a pace setting, haptics you can switch off, a length you choose.** Calm's Breathe Bubble offers 4, 6 or 8 breaths a minute, a haptics toggle, and separate volume for voice and background ([Calm support](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000069973-Calm-Breathing-Exercises-How-to-Adjust-Speed-Timers-Haptics-Vibrations)). → Our short-break screen gets one breathing shape, 6 breaths a minute by default, haptics off by default, and a reduced-motion version that counts in text. ^[inferred]
3. **A no-numbers mode, borrowed from Alto's Zen Mode.** Players wrote to the studio that they used the game to cope with anxiety. The studio's answer was a mode with no score, no coins and no interface, where a crash doesn't end the run ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/players-use-i-alto-s-adventure-i-as-coping-mechanism-dev-responds-with-zen-mode-), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2016-06-01-altos-adventure-zen-mode.html)). → A "quiet card" setting: same suggestion, same reason in words, with the numbers one tap away in the record rather than on the face. This is for ICP_5 at 1 am. ^[inferred]
4. **Escalate to a person: always there, offered when it fits, never a dead end.** Finch's "First Aid Kit" sits in the menu at all times and is also offered when you check in low ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)). Headspace's AI points to 988 when it detects risk, then ends the conversation ([Headspace AI](https://www.headspace.com/ai)). → Take Finch's always-there door and offer-when-low timing. Don't end the session: the escalate card keeps the break and the "not now" options. ^[inferred]
5. **Weekly recap: share the shape, never the data.** Wordle's share button was described at launch as a spoiler-free emoji grid. It shows how your game went and never gives away the answer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). → The weekly recap's share version shows the pattern of breaks (taken, skipped, snoozed) as a grid of shapes, with no sleep, heart rate or times. This extends S-12. ^[inferred]
6. **Once and done.** Wordle has one puzzle a day, about three minutes, and no ads or push notifications ([Time](https://time.com/6143715/wordle-sale-josh-wardle-interview)). Monument Valley was cut from 30 levels to 10 so that most players would finish it ([Wallpaper](https://www.wallpaper.com/tech/monument-valley-at-10-the-story-of-the-most-meticulous-puzzle-game-ever-created), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game))). → One suggestion per breakpoint. A break has a clear end screen ("Done. Back to it when you're ready"), not an endless feed. ^[inferred]
7. **Motion and sound: feedback that is felt more than heard.** Monument Valley 2 confirms each tap with a single randomised hang-drum note tuned to the level. Its composer used musical moments sparingly, where he wanted players to pause ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/behind-beautiful-sound-monument-valley-2-todd-baker/)). → Our app stays silent by default (rule 12 in [[09-design/design-evidence|Design evidence]]). A single soft tone or haptic is allowed only to mark the end of a break the student started, and it is opt-in. ^[inferred]
8. **No streaks, even "gentle" ones.** Calm sells a Daily Streaks tracker ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810)). A Finch reviewer said losing a 32-day streak stung ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)). The NYT had to apologise when Wordle streaks were lost in its migration ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). Even calm products ship streaks, and they hurt when they break. → Keep our no-streaks rule. History counts breaks that helped, not unbroken days. ^[inferred]

## How they talk to someone who's struggling

- **Offer, don't diagnose.** Headspace says its AI does not diagnose, treat or replace a therapist, and reminds members it is an AI ([Headspace AI](https://www.headspace.com/ai)). A Calm Health flyer says the app is not a substitute for a clinician and gives 988 for a crisis ([Western Health flyer](https://www.westernhealth.com/pdfs/shared/wellness-microsite/calm-health-app-flyer/)). → Our escalate copy says what we noticed and who can help. It never names a condition.
- **Plain emergency language, three options.** Headspace's help centre tells people worried about harming themselves or others to call 911, call or text 988, or go to the nearest ER ([Headspace help, via search snippet; the page returned 403 when fetched](https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/39817407791259-What-if-I-need-in-the-moment-support)). → This matches the ladder in [[09-design/design-evidence|Design evidence]]. Campus numbers must be confirmed and kept in config, never hard-coded.
- **Small steps for bad days.** Finch lets people set tiny goals (getting out of bed, taking medication) with no penalty for missing them ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html), [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748?see-all=reviews)). Reddit users objected when its "Tough Days" goal tab was renamed "Easy Wins", saying a shower isn't easy on some days ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1ssotmz/snapshots/2026-04-23T12%3A08%3A30.490679Z), low-quality mirror). → Never call the small step "easy". Say "small" or "one thing".
- **Acknowledge before you redirect.** Finch's bird reportedly answers journal entries with short replies that acknowledge the feeling ([Webfactory blog](https://www.webfactoryltd.com/how-to-talk-to-your-bird-in-the-finch-app/), `[unverified]`). Headspace's first session ends by asking how you feel ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/headspace)). → The escalate card's first line reflects the pattern ("Your sleep has been short most nights for two weeks") before any offer. ^[inferred]
- **Watch out for abrupt endings.** Ending a conversation once risk is detected (Headspace's AI) is safe for the company, but it can feel like being shown the door ^[inferred]. Our escalate state never closes the app or removes the other options.

## How We Feel

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** An emotion check-in journal from The How We Feel Project, Inc., a nonprofit. The product team is led by Pinterest co-founder Ben Silbermann and the science by Marc Brackett's Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence ([Yale Medicine](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/the-how-we-feel-app-helping-emotions-work-for-us-not-against-us/)). It's free and donation-funded, rated 4.9 from about 30K ratings, an Editors' Choice, on iPhone and Vision Pro, in English and Spanish ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384)). It was an Apple Design Award 2024 finalist for Social Impact ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2024/)).
- **Seen:** researched (App Store listing and reviews, Yale news, Apple editorial, Apple Design Awards page, newsletters).
- **Key screens and flows:** Check-in: pick one of four quadrants (energy × pleasantness), then a precise emotion word. Optionally tag what you were doing, who you were with and where, and add a note, photo or voice memo ([How Is](https://howisapp.substack.com/p/8-mood-tracking-apps-to-help-you), [Reysu](https://reysu.beehiiv.com/p/howwefeel)). One reviewer timed it at about 10 seconds ([Reysu](https://reysu.beehiiv.com/p/howwefeel)). The check-in then leads to strategies: roughly one-minute videos in four themes (change your thinking, move your body, mindfulness, reach out). Other features are patterns over time with HealthKit sleep and exercise, Friends, and a roughly 10-minute "Seasonal Snapshot" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Coarse first, then precise.** Two easy axes come before the vocabulary. A reviewer praised starting with "pleasant or unpleasant" ([How Is](https://howisapp.substack.com/p/8-mood-tracking-apps-to-help-you)).
  - **Each check-in is matched to an action.** A strategy is offered as a direct result of the check-in ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384)).
  - **Friends without a feed.** There are no likes and no browsing of others' history ([Reysu](https://reysu.beehiiv.com/p/howwefeel)).
  - **Data stays on the device by default.** Research sharing is opt-in ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384)).
- **Visual style:** Colour-coded by quadrant. Apple's editorial notes green for pleasant, lower-energy feelings, blue for less upbeat ones, and deeper shades for intensity ([Apple story](https://apps.apple.com/story/id1638393948)). Red and yellow for the high-energy quadrants follow the RULER Mood Meter `[unverified in-app]`. A word "wall" of emotions is reported ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384?see-all=reviews)). Motion and type are `[described]` only.
- **Copy and tone:** Warm and non-judgemental, as reviewers describe it ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384?see-all=reviews)). The founders' stance is that emotions are tools to manage, not good or bad ([Yale Medicine](https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/the-how-we-feel-app-helping-emotions-work-for-us-not-against-us/)). The vocabulary size is disputed: Brackett's page says 144 words ([marcbrackett.com](https://marcbrackett.com/how-we-feel-app-3/)), and a district newsletter claims over 500 `[unverified]`.
- **Crisis, escalation, wellness framing:** No in-app crisis flow was found in public sources `[unverified]`. Its "reach out" strategy theme is the closest analogue.
- **What users love / complain about:** People love that it's free with no paywall, the emotion vocabulary, the low-effort check-ins, and that therapists recommend it. They complain about iPad and iPhone not syncing, an opt-in AI feature (with privacy worries; the developer says entries don't train models), missing blended emotions such as grief, and a wish for more analytics ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384?see-all=reviews)).
- **Steal:**
  - **Two-axis first step, then words.** → Our optional "how do you feel?" tap after a break: two big buttons first, then three or four words. → History ("Did the breaks help?"). ^[inferred]
  - **Check-in matched to one strategy.** → If the student says "foggy", the next suggestion's reason mentions it. → Suggestion card reason line. ^[inferred]
  - **On-device by default, research opt-in.** → Mirrors our consent screen. → Onboarding and consent. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** Colour as the only carrier of meaning (it fails our Grey test). A Seasonal Snapshot style questionnaire (it reads as clinical screening). ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** Does it show crisis resources after repeated unpleasant check-ins? How are the HealthKit correlations worded (causal or not)?

## Finch

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A self-care app where you raise a bird by completing small goals. It's made by Finch Care Public Benefit Corporation, founded by two people who had lived with anxiety and depression ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748), [Paste](https://www.pastemagazine.com/article/finch-app-mental-health-virtual-pet-self-care)). It's free with Finch Plus and a "Guardian" sponsorship; in-app purchases run $5.99 to $69.99. It's rated 4.9 from about 772K ratings, an Editors' Choice, rated 9+ ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748)).
- **Seen:** researched (App Store listing and reviews, Engadget, Paste, the Pratt IxD critique, blogs, a Reddit mirror).
- **Key screens and flows:** Onboarding hatches and names the bird alongside a self-assessment. The Pratt critique says this overloads the first run ([Pratt IxD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)). Day to day: mood check-ins several times a day, a goal list, journaling prompts, and breathing. Completed goals give the bird energy for adventures and earn "rainbow stones" for cosmetics ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html), [Headway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Care for the pet stands in for care for yourself.** The pet never dies, unlike a Tamagotchi ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)).
  - **No penalty for missed goals beyond an optional reflection** ([Bear blog](https://chiltonm.bearblog.dev/everyone-i-know-is-addicted-to-the-bird-app/)). Streaks still reset ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)).
  - **The First Aid Kit** is offered when you check in low and is always in the menu. Finch links to a page of helplines ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)).
  - **Gentle social features.** Friends send "good vibes" (hugs, reminders to drink water) ([Bear blog](https://chiltonm.bearblog.dev/everyone-i-know-is-addicted-to-the-bird-app/)).
- **Visual style:** Cute, playful, a central bird, joyful reward animations ([Pratt IxD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)). Palette `[described]`.
- **Copy and tone:** Gentle and focused on what you did, not what you missed. Reviewers say a missed day feels like a pause ([Headway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)). Some find the cuteness childish ([search summary of reviews](https://www.autonomous.ai/en-GB/ourblog/finch-self-care-app-review-full-breakdown)).
- **Crisis, escalation, wellness framing:** First Aid Kit plus an external helpline page ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/this-self-care-virtual-pet-is-helping-me-get-my-act-together-160027169.html)). Whether it detects crisis language in journals is `[unverified]`.
- **What users love / complain about:** People love the tiny goals at their own pace, no punishment, inclusive customisation, and the Guardian programme for people who can't pay ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748?see-all=reviews)). Complaints: the paywall (critique and reviews), a cluttered home screen, rigid mood presets ([Pratt IxD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)), the reward loop turning into a chore, trial-to-yearly billing surprises ([Headway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/finch-app-review/)), and a compulsive pull to check the app ([Bear blog](https://chiltonm.bearblog.dev/everyone-i-know-is-addicted-to-the-bird-app/)).
- **Steal:**
  - **Help offered when low, and also always available.** → "Talk to someone" is a quiet link on every screen and is promoted only on persistence. → Escalate state. ^[inferred]
  - **Credit what was done.** → History leads with "3 breaks this week helped" rather than "you skipped 5". → History. ^[inferred]
  - **A missed thing is a pause.** → A declined suggestion produces no follow-up nag. → Declined state. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** The mascot and collectables (anti-steal: cartoon at serious moments). Variable rewards that pull people back to check. Calling small steps "easy". A first run that stacks setup and assessment. ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** What exactly does the First Aid Kit show at the moment of distress? Is there US-specific 988 routing?

## Headspace

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A meditation and sleep app that grew into coaching, therapy and an AI companion (Ebb) after its 2021 merger with Ginger ([It's Nice That](https://Itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)). $12.99 a month or $69.99 a year with a trial. Rated 4.8 from about 974K ratings, 13+. Its privacy label lists health data linked to you and used for advertising or marketing among other purposes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/headspace-meditation-sleep/id493145008)).
- **Seen:** researched (App Store listing and reviews, Headspace AI page, It's Nice That, teardowns).
- **Key screens and flows:** Onboarding (an Android teardown from 2022): sign in → feature carousel → "why are you here" picker whose animation and description change live with each option → a use-case screen → a subscribe screen before any session → about 9–10 taps to a 2-minute first session → "how are you feeling?" ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/headspace)). The Apple Watch app has a quick "panic button" per the editor's note ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/headspace-meditation-sleep/id493145008?see-all=reviews)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Breath-led animation** remains central to explaining ideas ([It's Nice That](https://Itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)). A third-party guide reconstructs a 4 s inhale, 2 s hold, 6 s exhale circle `[unverified]` ([Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace)).
  - **Live preview inside a choice.** The onboarding picker previews each option before you commit ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/headspace)).
- **Visual style:** The orange smile is kept. Since the 2024 rebrand (with Italic Studio, a custom Aperçu from Colophon, guidelines by Order), characters show stress, sadness and contentment, not only joy. The palette is chosen with accessibility in mind and positioned against the category's blues and greys ([It's Nice That](https://Itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)).
- **Copy and tone:** Friendly, aiming to destigmatise care. Critics question how a playful persona fits serious topics ([It's Nice That](https://Itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)).
- **Crisis, escalation, wellness framing:** Ebb screens every message in real time against seven risk categories (including suicidal ideation, self-harm and domestic violence). Flagged messages are de-identified and reviewed by licensed clinicians, who also audit a daily sample. On risk, Ebb gives a direct link to call or text 988, urges emergency care if needed, then ends the conversation. It is adults only and makes no diagnosis ([Headspace AI](https://www.headspace.com/ai)). For the deletion problems, see [[09-design/design-evidence|Design evidence]].
- **What users love / complain about:** People love the help with anxiety, panic and sleep, the breathing, and the teachers. They complain the library is overwhelming and want shuffle and rewind for sleepcasts ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/headspace-meditation-sleep/id493145008?see-all=reviews)). Trial-gate before value ([Tear Them Down](https://tearthemdown.substack.com/p/headspace)).
- **Steal:**
  - **Illustrations that can look sad.** → If we ever use an illustration, it must have non-happy states. The escalate card stays plain. → Visual system. ^[inferred]
  - **Live preview inside a choice.** → On the consent screen, toggling a data source previews the line it would add to a reason. → Onboarding and consent. ^[inferred]
  - **Short first session, then ask how it felt.** → The first suggestion ends with a one-tap "Did that help?". → First suggestion. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** A paywall or sign-in before any value. Ending the conversation at the moment of risk. Health data used for marketing. ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** How does Ebb word the moment of handover? What does the Watch panic button actually run?

## Calm

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A meditation, sleep and relaxation app founded in 2012 by Michael Acton Smith and Alex Tew ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Calm_(company))). Free with in-app purchases from about $14.99 to $79.99. Rated 4.8 from about 2M ratings, 9+, on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch, TV and Vision Pro. Its privacy label lists purchases, identifiers and usage data used to track you ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810)).
- **Seen:** researched (App Store listing and reviews, Calm support, Publishers Weekly, Trustpilot, onboarding galleries).
- **Key screens and flows:** One analysis says first run goes intro → a breathing exercise → a short meditation ([Screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/articles/calm-onboarding-design/)) `[described]`. Daily content includes Daily Calm, Sleep Stories, soundscapes, breathwork and 7- and 21-day programmes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Breathe Bubble.** Pace of 4, 6 or 8 breaths a minute, a haptics toggle, separate guidance and background volumes ([Calm support](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000069973-Calm-Breathing-Exercises-How-to-Adjust-Speed-Timers-Haptics-Vibrations)), and lengths from 1 minute to "Endless" ([search summary of Calm support](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000069973-Breathing-Exercises), page returned 403).
  - **Sleep Stories** (launched December 2016 with 23 stories) drop the usual arc. The engaging part comes first, then the story eases down, with no cliffhangers. They were designed with sleep psychologists to absorb racing thoughts ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20190819/80952-calm-is-sleeping-its-way-to-success.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Calm_(company))).
- **Visual style:** Full-bleed nature scenes ([UX Planet, 2018](https://uxplanet.org/5-classic-mobile-onboarding-examples-from-top-apps-of-2018-cdbeebcb2a38)) `[described]`.
- **Copy and tone:** Low-drama and understated, especially in narration ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20190819/80952-calm-is-sleeping-its-way-to-success.html)). Reviewers praise particular narrators' voices ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810?see-all=reviews)).
- **Crisis, escalation, wellness framing:** No in-app crisis flow was found. The employer product (Calm Health) says it's not a substitute for a clinician and points to 988 ([Western Health flyer](https://www.westernhealth.com/pdfs/shared/wellness-microsite/calm-health-app-flyer/)). The consumer app's crisis handling is `[unverified]`.
- **What users love / complain about:** People love the help with anxiety and insomnia, Sleep Stories, and narrators. Complaints: content moved behind the paywall, stories removed, update bugs ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calm/id571800810?see-all=reviews)), and auto-renew and cancellation trouble ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.calm.com), self-selected).
- **Steal:**
  - **Pace choice for breathing**, with haptics as a toggle. → Short-break screen. ^[inferred]
  - **Engaging first, then wind down.** → The break screen gives its one instruction at the top, then gets quieter: fewer words, slower motion, no new information. → Break in progress. ^[inferred]
  - **Separate volume for voice and background.** → If we add audio later, separate it the same way. → Break settings. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** Streak trackers. Tracking-purpose analytics in a wellness app. Celebrity voice as the selling point. "Endless" as a break length, because our breaks end. ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** Does the consumer app show crisis lines anywhere? What are the default pace and haptics settings?

## Alto's Odyssey

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A one-touch sandboarding game by Team Alto (Harry Nesbitt, Ryan Cash, Eli Cymet), published by Snowman. $4.99, no ads or in-app purchases, rated 4+, Apple Design Award 2018 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/altos-odyssey/id1182456409), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto%27s_Odyssey)).
- **Seen:** researched (App Store, Wikipedia, Engadget, Game Developer, TNW).
- **Key screens and flows:** Tap to jump, hold to trick. Dynamic weather and light (sandstorms, shooting stars). A Photo Mode from pause. Zen Mode is a separate entry with its own soundtrack ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/altos-odyssey/id1182456409)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Zen Mode** (first added to Alto's Adventure in 2016) came from player emails about using the game to calm down. It removes score, power-ups and the interface ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/players-use-i-alto-s-adventure-i-as-coping-mechanism-dev-responds-with-zen-mode-)). You can get back up after a crash ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto%27s_Odyssey)).
  - **Taking things out.** The producer took Zen Mode's reception as proof that removing features can beat adding them ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-altos-odyssey-transports-players-to-a-whole-new-world)).
- **Visual style:** Layered silhouettes and atmospheric light `[described]`. The original deliberately reduced visual complexity compared with other endless runners ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-altos-odyssey-transports-players-to-a-whole-new-world)).
- **Copy and tone:** Almost wordless in Zen Mode ^[inferred].
- **Crisis, escalation, wellness framing:** None, and none is needed. The wellness framing came from players.
- **What users love / complain about:** Critics praised the atmosphere, but said it was too similar to the first game and had a coin grind ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto%27s_Odyssey)).
- **Steal:**
  - **Same product, minus the numbers.** → A "quiet card" setting. → Suggestion card settings. ^[inferred]
  - **A crash doesn't end the run.** → A declined break doesn't reset anything. → Declined state. ^[inferred]
  - **Listen to how people actually use you.** → Pilot interviews ask "when did you open it without a prompt?". → Pilot metric. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** Grind loops (coins) in the main mode.
- **Open questions:** What share of players use Zen Mode? Not public.

## Monument Valley

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A puzzle game from ustwo games, led by Ken Wong, 2014. Ten levels of impossible architecture with the silent princess Ida. Apple Design Award 2014, BAFTA wins, Metacritic 89. Sequels in 2017 and 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game))). The current platform and pricing (including any Netflix distribution) is `[unverified]`.
- **Seen:** researched (Wikipedia, Wallpaper, Designer Fund, GDC listing, A Sound Effect, Engadget).
- **Key screens and flows:** Tap to move Ida, twist handles and slide platforms to reshape paths. Colour hints at what is interactive ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game))). It's chaptered and short: reviewers estimated one to three hours.
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **Built to be finished.** The team chose a short game most people could complete over a long, hard one. They cut 30 planned levels to 10 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game)), [Wallpaper](https://www.wallpaper.com/tech/monument-valley-at-10-the-story-of-the-most-meticulous-puzzle-game-ever-created)). Many non-gamers wrote that it was the first game they ever finished ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-08-11-monument-valley-recouped-costs-in-one-week.html)).
  - **The game designed as UX.** Being inside a UX studio shaped it, and every screen was meant to work as a print you'd hang on a wall ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-i-monument-valley-i-to-be-an-experience-not-a-game), [Designer Fund](https://designerfund.com/blog/secrets-behind-the-success-of-monument-valley)). Respecting players meant minimising frustration ([Designer Fund](https://designerfund.com/blog/secrets-behind-the-success-of-monument-valley)).
  - **Sound as feedback** (MV2). Moving platforms carry musical layers, a solved puzzle plays a short sting, and taps play tuned hang-drum notes, used sparingly ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/behind-beautiful-sound-monument-valley-2-todd-baker/)).
- **Visual style:** Axonometric and pixel-precise, drawing on Escher, Japanese prints and minimalist sculpture. Level framing was compared to flower arranging ([Wallpaper](https://www.wallpaper.com/tech/monument-valley-at-10-the-story-of-the-most-meticulous-puzzle-game-ever-created), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game))).
- **Copy and tone:** Sparse, and largely wordless ^[inferred from the "silent" protagonist and absence of tutorials in coverage; `[unverified]`].
- **What users love / complain about:** Critics loved the art, sound and puzzles, but said it was too easy, too short, and the story vague ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Valley_(video_game))).
- **Steal:**
  - **Finishable by design.** → A break is a finite object with a visible end. → Break in progress and break done. ^[inferred]
  - **Every frame composed.** → Test each card state as a still. If a screenshot of "not enough data" looks broken, it is broken. → All card states. ^[inferred]
  - **Colour marks only what you can touch.** → The accent colour is reserved for the one action. → Visual system. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** Optical illusions or cleverness on Operate surfaces. Save that for the Persuade surfaces (the showcase page) only. ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** Does the game have haptics? Not found in sources.

## Wordle

- **What it is · who · platform · price:** A daily five-letter word game by Josh Wardle, made for his partner. Public in late 2021. Bought by The New York Times on 31 January 2022 for a reported low seven figures. Free on the web and in the NYT Games app ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)).
- **Seen:** researched (Wikipedia, Time interview, NBC and news coverage).
- **Key screens and flows:** Six guesses. Tiles turn green, yellow or grey. Hard mode. A high-contrast mode swaps to orange and blue. Then statistics and Share ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)).
- **Mechanisms:**
  - **One puzzle a day, the same for everyone.** Wardle described the scarcity as keeping it to about three minutes a day. There are no ads or push notifications built to bring people back ([Time](https://time.com/6143715/wordle-sale-josh-wardle-interview)).
  - **A share that leaks nothing.** The emoji grid came from players in New Zealand, and Wardle adopted their format ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)).
  - **Stewardship after the sale.** The NYT kept it free, cast itself as stewards ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/f4a912c80d264e73a7909a0a64b232e9-DWz89X_2/)), removed obscure and sensitive words (for example "fetus" after the Dobbs leak), and appointed a dedicated editor in November 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)).
- **Visual style:** Flat tiles, three states, a high-contrast alternative ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)).
- **Copy and tone:** Nearly none. The game is the copy ^[inferred].
- **What users love / complain about:** Lost streaks after the migration, fears that the NYT made it harder (denied by its games chief), and division over the editor's word choices ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle), [NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/entertainment/entertainment-news/is-wordle-getting-harder-fans-have-questions-after-game-acquired-by-the-new-york-times/2815319/)).
- **Steal:**
  - **Spoiler-free share.** → The weekly recap shares the shape of a week, never health values. → Recap (extends S-12). ^[inferred]
  - **High-contrast alternative to colour.** → Every state also has a shape or word. → Grey test. ^[inferred]
  - **A word editor's sensitivity pass.** → A wording review of every reason string, for unintended meanings to a struggling reader. → Copy process. ^[inferred]
- **Avoid:** Streak statistics, because they turned a calm game into something you can lose. ^[inferred]
- **Open questions:** None that block us.

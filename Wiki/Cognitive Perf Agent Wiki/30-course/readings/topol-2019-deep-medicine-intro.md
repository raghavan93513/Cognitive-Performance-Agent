# Topol 2019 — Deep Medicine (Introduction)

> [!cite] Eric Topol. *Deep Medicine: How Artificial Intelligence Can Make Healthcare Human Again* (introduction / chapter 1). Basic Books, 2019. Course copy on CourseWorks (class use only).
> Assigned in [[30-course/weeks/week-01-personal-health-data-foundations|Week 1 — Personal Health Data Foundations]].

> [!warning] Not read directly
> The book PDF is on CourseWorks, and the Week 1 slides say Dr. Topol allowed its use only within the class, so it must not be shared outside the course (slides p. 15). We did not access it. Apart from the slide quote, everything below comes from general knowledge of the book and is marked ^[inferred].

## Core idea

- The Week 1 slides quote Topol's chapter 1. In paraphrase: AI's greatest opportunity is not fewer errors, lighter workloads or even curing disease. It is restoring the trust and human connection between patients and doctors (slides p. 15).
- Topol's frame is "shallow medicine": rushed visits, misdiagnosis and overtreatment. Deep phenotyping of each person, deep learning and **deep empathy** together could make care more personal. ^[inferred]
- The book's opening uses personal and patient stories to show how care fails when clinicians have no time and no full picture of the patient. ^[inferred]

## Key findings

- It is a book, not a study. Its main claims ^[inferred]:
  - Medicine is short of time and full of errors, and AI could take over the clerical and pattern-recognition work.
  - The time saved has to go back into the patient relationship, or AI will just speed up the treadmill.
  - Data that individuals generate (wearables, sensors, genomics) is part of a "deep" picture of each person.
  - Bias, privacy and hype are real risks.

## Why the course assigned it

- Week 1 aims to give the big picture of personal health assistants. Topol supplies the purpose: AI should strengthen the human side of care, not replace it. The slides then define the course goal as a PHA whose outputs are personalised, dynamic, inspectable and safe (slides pp. 14–16). ^[inferred]
- It pairs with [[30-course/readings/estrin-2014-small-data-n-of-me|Estrin 2014]]: Estrin covers the data side (n = me), Topol the care and relationship side. ^[inferred]

## What this means for us

- Our agent should **support the student's own judgement and routines**, not take control. That matches the accept / snooze / decline loop in [[08-frontend/student-interface-and-feedback|student interface and feedback]]. ^[inferred]
- "Restore the human touch" here means fewer, better-timed nudges rather than constant ones. That is an argument for conservative recommendation rates in [[04-agent-engine/multi-agent-decision|multi-agent decision]]. ^[inferred]
- Topol's optimism should be read alongside the deployment-reality readings ([[30-course/readings/kelly-2019-clinical-impact-ai|Kelly 2019]], [[30-course/readings/beede-2020-human-centered-eval-deployed-dl|Beede 2020]]). ^[inferred]

# Topol 2019 — High-Performance Medicine

> [!cite] Eric J. Topol. *High-performance medicine: the convergence of human and artificial intelligence.* Nature Medicine, 2019 (optional reading). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-018-0300-7
> Assigned (optional) in [[30-course/weeks/week-14-final-showcase|Week 14 — Final Showcase]].

## Core idea

- Deep learning in medicine has been enabled by labelled big data, much more computing power and cloud storage.
- AI is starting to have impact at **three levels**:
  - **Clinicians:** mainly through fast, accurate **image interpretation**.
  - **Health systems:** better **workflow** and possibly fewer medical errors.
  - **Patients:** helping people **process their own data to promote health**.
- **Current limitations:** **bias, privacy and security, and lack of transparency.**

## Key findings

- Topol expects marked improvements in accuracy, productivity and workflow over time. Whether these **strengthen or erode the patient–doctor relationship** remains to be seen (from the abstract).
- From general knowledge of the review ^[inferred]:
  - It catalogues many deep-learning studies across radiology, pathology, dermatology, ophthalmology and cardiology. ^[inferred]
  - It notes how few were prospective or done in real clinical settings. ^[inferred]
  - It discusses patient-facing algorithms on phones and wearables, together with their validation gaps. ^[inferred]

- Week 14 is the final showcase. This optional review closes the course where Week 1's [[30-course/readings/topol-2019-deep-medicine-intro|Deep Medicine]] opened it: with Topol's framing of AI for clinicians, systems and **patients themselves**. ^[inferred]

## What this means for us

- **We sit at Topol's "patient" level:** helping individuals use their own data. That framing (empowerment, not diagnosis) is a good one-line pitch for the [[30-course/deliverables/part-3-final-showcase|final showcase]]. ^[inferred]

Halftime: three directions for the suggestion card

The card that tells a student, mid-study, whether to keep going, take a short walk or take a break, and shows why and how sure it is. Pick a state, compare the three live phones, then choose a direction. The name "Halftime" is the CMO seat's working pick, not final.

UIUX seat (design) and CMO seat (words) · 5 Nov 2026 · iPhone 390×844 · sample data only, no real person's numbers

The designer's pick: A, with C as its night mode

A "Clear Card" is the fastest one-glance read: the suggestion as a verb, the reason in the student's own units with a source marker on every clause, and "how sure" as a word plus a count. It's also the most iPhone-native of the three. Keep going is as big and easy as the break, because in the one sec study (PNAS 2023) an easy way out did more than any persuasive wording.

C "Quiet" becomes A's appearance after dark: same words, same buttons, on a calm near-black ground with a serif headline. That's the 1 am student (persona Sam) without a second product to build.

B's session line is too good to lose but too busy for the card face. Move it into "See why" and the history screen, where the student has asked for detail.

Strongest argument against

A's big session clock could read as pressure ("you've been at it 2:40"). If the classmate tests show that, shrink it to one line and let the card carry the time.

To decide as a team

  1. Which direction: A, B, C, or A with C at night?
  2. Voice: the CMO recommends the calm-friend voice A, short on the card face (copy in COPY.md).
  3. Name: Halftime, Pace, Breather, Seam or Steady. Trademarks need checking before anything public.
  4. What "a walk helped" counts: the student's own "It helped" answer after a break, or their practice score afterwards. The CTO and CSO decide.
  5. Crisis numbers: placeholders until confirmed with Columbia Health.

Checked so far

  • All 10 states × 3 directions shot on iPhone SE, standard iPhone and Pro Max: no failed UI checks, no console errors.
  • Not checked yet: real Safari on an iPhone, VoiceOver, how the motion feels, and real classmates.

These are proposals for the Nov 6 proposal, not final designs. Every number is synthetic. No suggestion here is medical advice, and the app never contacts anyone for the student. Built from the team's design studies and steal library (Google Flights' usual-range bar, Perplexity-style source markers, Citymapper's one next step with equal alternatives, Apple Vitals' speak-only-when-signals-agree).
